Copy, Content & Language

A comprehensive guide to writing like SassyMartie — warm, direct, honest, and occasionally funny. For anyone writing copy, creating content, or building human-facing interactions in the SassyMartie universe.

SECTION 01

Voice & Tone

Voice is who we are. Tone is how we adjust to different situations. The SassyMartie voice is consistent — warm, direct, and human. The tone shifts slightly depending on context, but never loses that core personality.

The One-Sentence Brief
Talk like the friendly senior who remembers what it felt like to be new — warm, direct, no fluff, occasionally funny but never trying hard.
Core Voice Attributes
Warm but not gushing. Like a friend who's happy you showed up — but won't make it weird. The welcome is genuine, not performed.

Direct but not cold. Short sentences. Say the thing. Don't bury the point inside passive constructions or three-clause qualifiers.

Honest, not hype. Don't oversell. Trust that the right people will get it. Hyperbole is the enemy of credibility.
SECTION 02

The Martie Test

Before publishing any copy, design, or decision, apply one question: Would Martie say this to a new friend at a hackathon?

✓ IF YES
Ship it. The copy feels natural, genuine, and helpful. It sounds like something a real person would say.
✗ SOUNDS LIKE A PRESS RELEASE
Rewrite it. Strip out the corporate language, the passive voice, and the buzzwords. Make it human.
✓
Read the copy out loud. If it sounds weird when spoken, it's probably too formal or robotic.
✓
Remove any sentence that could appear in a corporate memo or government form.
✓
If you're using more than one exclamation mark, you're trying too hard. One, rarely. None, usually.
✓
Check for words from the "Never Use" list. Replace them with simpler, more human alternatives.
SECTION 03

Voice Pillars

These five principles guide every piece of copy, every interaction, every message. When in doubt, return to these pillars.

1. WARM BUT NOT GUSHING
Like a friend who's happy you showed up — but won't make it weird. The welcome is genuine, not performed.
✓ DO
"Hey, glad you're here."
"Your tool belongs here too."
✗ DON'T
"Welcome to our AMAZING community of INCREDIBLE creators!!!"
Too much enthusiasm feels fake and exhausting.
2. DIRECT BUT NOT COLD
Short sentences. Say the thing. Don't bury the point inside passive constructions or three-clause qualifiers.
✓ DO
"List your tool. Tell people what it does. Done."
✗ DON'T
"Our platform facilitates the seamless submission and discoverability of your software product."
This sounds like enterprise software documentation, not a helpful friend.
3. HONEST, NOT HYPE
Don't oversell. Trust that the right people will get it. Hyperbole is the enemy of credibility.
✓ DO
"We're small but growing. And we're building this with you."
✗ DON'T
"The #1 AI SaaS directory trusted by thousands of world-class founders!"
This is marketing hype. It erodes trust instead of building it.
4. LIGHT HUMOR, NEVER PUNCHING DOWN
The joke is always about the shared experience — the chaos of building, the too-many-ideas situation, the cold coffee. Never at the user's expense.
✓ DO
"Too many ideas, not enough time. We know."
"No pitch deck needed."
✗ DON'T
Sarcasm that makes the user feel foolish or alienates newcomers.
Humor should build connection, not create distance.
5. ASSUMES COMPETENCE
Don't over-explain. The audience is smart. Trust them to know what a URL is.
✓ DO
"Add your product in under 5 minutes."
✗ DON'T
"First, you'll need to navigate to our submission portal where you can begin the process of..."
This treats the user like they've never used a website before.
SECTION 04

Buttons & CTAs

Primary actions are verb-first, short, and purposeful. Secondary actions are equally clear, just lower urgency. Never use corporate jargon or passive constructions in CTAs.

PRIMARY CTAs
Use: "List your tool" · "Get discovered" · "Join" · "Ship it"
Verb-first, action-oriented, clear benefit. No fluff.
✓ GOOD
List your tool
Get discovered
Join the directory
Start building
✗ BAD
Click here to submit
Apply now
Get started today!
Submit your application
These are either too generic, too formal, or trying too hard with exclamation marks.
SECONDARY CTAs
Use: "See how it works" · "Browse tools" · "Learn more"
Still clear, just lower urgency than primary actions.
DESTRUCTIVE ACTIONS
Use: "Remove this" · "Delete" · "Cancel"
Don't over-explain or add scary language. Keep it simple and direct.
✓ GOOD
Delete this listing
✗ BAD
Permanently delete this item forever
"Permanently" and "forever" are redundant and dramatic. Just say "Delete."
EMPTY STATE CTAs
Use: "Add your first tool" · "Create something" · "Start here"
Inviting and low-pressure. No negative language.
✓ GOOD
Add your first tool
✗ BAD
No items found. Please add items.
"No items found" sounds like an error. Make it inviting instead.
SECTION 05

Placeholder Text

Give a real hint. Don't just repeat the field label. The placeholder should help the user understand what format or style of answer is expected.

TOOL NAME
Field label: Tool Name
Placeholder: "What do people call it?"
✓ GOOD
What do people call it?
✗ BAD
Name
Enter name
Tool name here
These just repeat the label. Not helpful.
DESCRIPTION
Field label: Description
Placeholder: "What does it do? Keep it human."
URL
Field label: Website
Placeholder: "Where do people find it?"
✓ GOOD
Where do people find it?
✗ BAD
https://
Enter URL
Too technical and unhelpful.
CATEGORY / DROPDOWN
Field label: Category
Placeholder: "Pick the closest fit"
EMAIL
Field label: Your Email
Placeholder: "We'll send you updates here"
Explain why you need it, not just what format.
SECTION 06

State Messages

State messages are how you communicate system status to users — empty states, errors, success, loading. Keep them calm, honest, and human.

EMPTY STATE
"Nothing here yet."
Optional addition: "Could be your tool."
✓ GOOD
Nothing here yet. Could be yours though.
✗ BAD
No results found. Try adjusting your filters or search criteria.
Too robotic and instructional.
ERROR MESSAGE
"Something went wrong. Try again?"
"That link doesn't seem right. Double-check it?"
Honest, calm, helpful. No blame.
✓ GOOD
Something went wrong on our end. Try again in a sec.
✗ BAD
Error 500: Internal Server Error. The server encountered an unexpected condition.
Technical jargon doesn't help the user fix anything.
SUCCESS MESSAGE
"You're listed. Go tell someone."
"Saved. ♡"
"Done. Your tool is live."
Genuine celebration without going overboard.
✓ GOOD
You're in. Go build.
✗ BAD
Congratulations! Your submission has been successfully processed!!!
Too much enthusiasm feels fake.
LOADING STATE
"Finding the good stuff..."
"One sec."
"On it."
Short and casual. No need to explain what's happening under the hood.
404 PAGE
"Hmm. That page doesn't exist. Maybe it moved. Maybe it never did."
Slightly philosophical, but still helpful. Include a link back to home or search.
SECTION 07

Onboarding Copy

Onboarding should feel like a checklist a friend wrote — not a form in a government portal. Keep step labels conversational and encouraging.

STEP LABELS
Step 1: "Tell us about your tool"
Step 2: "Add a link"
Step 3: "You're done"
✓ GOOD
Tell us about your tool
Add a link
You're done
✗ BAD
Step 1: Product Information Entry
Step 2: URL Submission
Step 3: Complete
This sounds like enterprise software, not a friendly directory.
WELCOME MESSAGE
"Let's get your thing listed. It won't take long."
Warm, direct, sets expectations without being formal.
COMPLETION MESSAGE
"You're in. Go build."
Short celebration, then action. No confetti needed.
SECTION 08

Marketing Copy

Marketing copy should be confident and punchy without sliding into hype. Focus on the benefit, not the features. Speak to the feeling, not just the function.

Homepage Hero Example

Get your AI tool discovered.
Built different. Listed here.

SassyMartie is the curated directory for indie founders building AI-powered SaaS. No corporate fluff. No pitch deck required. Just ship it and get seen.

✓ GOOD
Built different. Listed here.

The directory for scrappy builders who ship real things for real people.
✗ BAD
The #1 AI SaaS Discovery Platform

Revolutionizing how world-class founders connect with enterprise buyers through our cutting-edge directory ecosystem.
Buzzword soup. This sounds like every startup pitch deck.
Key Principles for Marketing Copy
Lead with the benefit, not the feature. "Get discovered" beats "Directory listing functionality."

Use concrete language. "Built by indie hackers" beats "Community-driven platform."

Don't claim #1 unless you can prove it. And even then, probably don't.

One CTA per page. Don't scatter "Sign up" buttons everywhere.
TAGLINES & SHORT COPY
"Discover. Connect. Support."
"Built with heart, chaos, and caffeine."
"Too many ideas. Ship the one that matters."
"No pitch deck needed."
SECTION 09

Articles & Blog Posts

Articles should feel like Martie wrote them — conversational, helpful, and structured for scannability. No corporate speak. No unnecessarily long intros.

How to Write a Great Tool Description (That Actually Gets Clicks)

Your tool description has one job: make someone curious enough to click through. That's it. Not to explain every feature. Not to list every use case. Just enough to spark interest.

Here's the thing — most people scan. They're not reading every word of every listing. They're looking for the one that feels like it solves their specific problem right now. So your description needs to be clear, specific, and human.

Start with the problem. What does your tool fix? "Automates cold email outreach" is better than "AI-powered sales enablement platform." The first one tells me what it does. The second one could be anything.

Keep it short. Two sentences is usually enough. Three if you need to clarify something. If you're writing a paragraph, you've lost them.

Skip the buzzwords. "Leverage" and "synergy" and "ecosystem" don't help. They just make your tool sound like every other tool. Use plain language.

✓
Lead with the benefit or problem. Don't bury it in the third paragraph.
✓
Use short paragraphs. 2-3 sentences max. Walls of text are intimidating.
✓
Use subheadings. Make it easy to scan. Every section should have a clear topic.
✓
End with a clear next step. What should the reader do after reading? Tell them.
✓
Read it out loud. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.
SECTION 10

Email Templates

Emails should feel personal, not automated. Subject lines are direct or curious — never clickbait. Body copy is short paragraphs, one CTA, and a human sign-off.

Your listing is live ♡
From: SassyMartie

Hey there,

Your tool is now live on SassyMartie. People can find it, click through, and (hopefully) start using it.

Want to increase your chances of getting discovered? Share your listing on Twitter or LinkedIn. The more eyes on it, the better.

Good luck building. ♡

— Martie
SassyMartie
✓ GOOD SUBJECT LINES
Your listing is live ♡
Someone found your tool today
Quick question about your listing
Your monthly summary
✗ BAD SUBJECT LINES
You won't BELIEVE what happened!!!
URGENT: Action Required
Re: Your SassyMartie Account Status
[Newsletter] October 2026 Edition
Too clickbaity, too formal, or too boring.
Someone found your tool today
From: SassyMartie

Quick heads up —

Your tool got 12 clicks in the past 24 hours. Not bad. That's 12 people who were curious enough to check it out.

Want to keep that momentum going? Update your listing with a fresh screenshot or a clearer description. Small tweaks can make a difference.

— Martie
Email Writing Guidelines
Subject line: Direct, curious, or news-shaped. Never clickbait.

Opening: "Hey" or "Quick heads up" — not "Dear valued user."

Body: Short paragraphs. One main point. One CTA.

Sign-off: "— Martie" not "Sincerely, The SassyMartie Team."

Platform-Specific Guidelines
Twitter / X: Short, punchy, honest. No hashtag spam. One thought per tweet. Heart at the end when it earns it.

Instagram: Illustration-first. Caption is secondary. Don't explain the image — react to it.

LinkedIn: Still SassyMartie — but slightly less casual. No buzzwords. Builder stories, not growth metrics.

Threads / Bluesky: Most casual. This is where Martie can ramble a little. Builder hot takes welcome.
✓
Never use engagement bait. "Comment YES if you agree" is banned.
✓
Don't post growth metrics as personality. "We hit 1,000 users!" is not interesting content.
✓
No competitor callouts or shade. Build your own thing, don't tear down others.
✓
If it sounds like AI wrote it, rewrite it. No one wants to read robot copy.

SECTION 14

Good vs Bad Examples

The best way to learn the voice is to see it in action. Here are real examples of good copy vs the kind of copy that misses the mark.

SCENARIO: HOMEPAGE HERO
✓ GOOD
Get your AI tool discovered.

SassyMartie is the curated directory for indie founders building AI-powered SaaS. Ship it. List it. Get seen.
Clear benefit. Simple language. Action-oriented.
✗ BAD
Revolutionizing AI SaaS Discovery

Our cutting-edge platform leverages proprietary algorithms to facilitate seamless connections between world-class founders and enterprise stakeholders.
Buzzword soup. Vague. Sounds like every other startup.
SCENARIO: EMPTY SEARCH RESULTS
✓ GOOD
Nothing matches that search. Try something broader?
Calm. Helpful. Doesn't blame the user.
✗ BAD
0 results found. Please refine your search query and try again.
Robotic. Sounds like an error message, not helpful guidance.
SCENARIO: TOOL SUBMISSION CONFIRMATION
✓ GOOD
You're listed. Your tool is live and people can find it now. Go tell someone. ♡
Genuine celebration. Clear next step. Human touch.
✗ BAD
Congratulations!!! Your submission has been successfully processed and is now live in our directory!!!
Too many exclamation marks. Sounds automated and fake.
SCENARIO: ABOUT PAGE
✓ GOOD
SassyMartie was built because good tools kept getting buried. The indie builder's problem isn't talent or effort — it's visibility. So we built the place to look.
Honest. Tells the story. Explains the why.
✗ BAD
Founded in 2026, SassyMartie is a mission-driven organization committed to empowering the global AI SaaS ecosystem through innovative discovery solutions.
Corporate jargon. Sounds like a press release, not a real story.
SECTION 15

Real Scenarios

Here are complete examples of how to handle common writing situations — from feature announcements to support responses.

SCENARIO: NEW FEATURE ANNOUNCEMENT (EMAIL)
Something new: Featured listings
From: SassyMartie

Quick update —

We added featured listings. If you want your tool to show up at the top of the directory for a week, you can now do that. It's $49. One payment. No recurring charges.

Why would you do this? More visibility. More clicks. More people checking out your thing.

Not required. Not pushy. Just there if you want it.

— Martie
SCENARIO: RESPONDING TO SUPPORT REQUEST
✓ GOOD RESPONSE
Hey —

Just checked your listing. The link issue was on our end — fixed it. Should work now.

Let me know if it's still acting weird.

— Martie
Direct. Takes responsibility. Offers to follow up.
✗ BAD RESPONSE
Dear User,

Thank you for contacting SassyMartie Support. We have reviewed your ticket (#2847) and resolved the reported issue. Please allow 24-48 hours for changes to propagate.

Best regards,
The SassyMartie Team
Robotic. Formal. Sounds like an automated system, not a person.
SCENARIO: PRICING PAGE COPY

Simple Pricing

Free forever: List your tool. Get discovered. No credit card needed.

Featured listing ($49 one-time): Boost your tool to the top for 7 days. More visibility. More clicks. No recurring fees.

That's it. No hidden charges. No enterprise plans. Just two options.

SCENARIO: 404 ERROR PAGE

Hmm. That page doesn't exist.

Maybe it moved. Maybe it never did. Either way, it's not here.

Try this: Go back to the homepage or browse all tools.

Key Takeaway
Every piece of copy — from CTAs to error messages to emails — should pass the Martie Test. Would she say this to a friend at a hackathon? If not, rewrite it.
